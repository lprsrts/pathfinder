# Changelog

What changed in each release of Pathfinder, newest first. `pathfinder update` shows the newest section.

## Unreleased

- **"Every part is resolved" agrees with the parts count.** The reply said "Every part is resolved." while the rule showed `Parts 2/7`, which counts only parts you understood. The count now says so (`Understood 2/7` on the rule, `2/7 understood` in the side panel), and the reply says how each part was resolved: `Every part is resolved: 2 understood, 5 handed off without understanding.`
- **Review checks read off · light · full.** The highest level was shown as `normal`, which read as the middle one. It is now shown as `full`; the stored value is still `normal`, so saved settings are unchanged.
- **A check passed in this folder counts in the task's plan.** After a quick check passed on the Gaussian product, the task's map still read `Rests on: Gaussian probability densities (unknown), Gaussian mixtures and their weights (unknown), …`: the Mapper named the ideas anew instead of reusing the folder's concepts, so the pass never counted. The Mapper now sees this folder's concepts with your state on each (`gaussian-product: Product of two Gaussians (shown)`), when it builds a map and now also when it proposes a change, and must reuse an id when a part rests on that idea. A new name is still linked to the recorded concept when its name, or now the words of its id, say the same thing. Another folder's concepts are never shown or counted.
- **Delegating a part no longer marks shared ideas as delegated.** Handing off the random inputs marked every concept that part rested on as `delegated`, including the Gaussian densities the next part still rests on. A concept another open part rests on now keeps its own state until you hand off every part that rests on it.
- **Math reads as math, not LaTeX.** Replies and the agent's report showed raw LaTeX such as `\(c_{ij}=\mathcal N(\mu_{a_i};\mu_{b_j},v_{a_i}+v_{b_j})\)` and `\(2.22\times10^{-16}\)`. Every model whose prose you read (onboarding, the map, the Tutor, candidates, the examiner, the Judge, `/tell`, the planted check and the agent) is now asked for plain Unicode math, and LaTeX that still comes is drawn as Unicode: `cᵢⱼ = 𝒩(μ_(aᵢ); μ_(bⱼ), v_(aᵢ) + v_(bⱼ))`, `2.22×10⁻¹⁶`, `(a + b)/2`, `(1/2)mv²`, `√(x)`, `∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ`, `| a b; c d |`. The rendering never changes what the math says: fractions keep their grouping, nested scripts stay nested, matrices keep their brackets and anything unknown is kept as written. Code and quotes from sources stay verbatim, prices like `$5` and shell variables like `$HOME/$USER` stay as written, and what is stored and gated is unchanged; the gate's word-for-word pre-check now reads both the text and its drawn form, so writing a key's math in LaTeX doesn't slip past it.
- **Citations hold on math pages.** In a LaTeX preprint, the cites the model found for Proposition B.3 (p.29), §6.8 (p.18) and equation (39) (p.27) were all dropped, and the reply fell back to generic lines. Those pages' big operators come out as letters (CMEX draws ∑ as `X`), so no line on them could be quoted. Now poppler's account of which runs hold such letters is kept, and a plain prose line whose words lie outside them can be quoted or found (a heading, a proposition's title, a sentence with `[18]`), while a wrong letter inside a sentence (`forP one`) never is. A quote that runs into math is shown by its own prose, and a quote of the math itself (from the text, or as LaTeX or Unicode) by the line that introduces it, such as `Proposition A.6 (Bayesian Fusion of Gaussians).` for equation (39). Garbled symbols are still never shown as a quote, a made-up formula (`price = cost + margin`) is never placed by the letters it shares with a page's math, and a real phrase cut from a quote never vouches for made-up words around it. The log now names the quote of a dropped cite.
- **`/find` puts the exact phrase first.** `/find Bayesian Fusion for GMMs` listed the first 12 of 145 lines holding any of its words, and not the heading `Proposition B.3 (Bayesian Fusion for GMMs).` first. Lines holding the words as one phrase now come first, then lines with every word; `for`, `of` and other short words count only toward the phrase, and when some lines hold every word only those are listed and counted (2 instead of 145 there).
- **`/web` lists each paper once.** A paper's title and a line of its abstract from the same address were two entries (`§ Title` and `§ Abstract`); they are now one, the abstract's line. One arXiv paper's `abs/…v1` and `html/…` pages (and its `pdf/`, other versions and arXiv DOI) are one source too, and so is a DOI served from different resolvers.
- **A caught check is recorded as caught.** After an objection caught a planted flaw, `/why` said the part was handed off "and you approved it", and the reply's "You caught it" line scrolled out of sight above the agent's report. The record now says `you caught the planted check and objected, so the agent's real result was written`, and "You caught it: …" opens the reply, before the agent's report and the next part's card.
- **A check passed in one thread counts in the others.** A concept checked in the folder's chat (a quick check) wasn't credited in a task thread of the same folder when the task's map named it in other words, so the plan said "you haven't shown L2 distance between Gaussian mixtures yet" right after that check passed. A new map's concept is now the recorded one when its name says the same in other words (`L2 distance of two GMMs`, `L2 distance between Gaussian mixtures`), the map is told to reuse recorded ids from every thread, and the decision model matching names counts an abbreviation or rewording as the same idea. Swapping a relation's arguments is never the same idea (`probability of X given Y` is not `probability of Y given X`).
- **"Do the rest" gives the agent every part.** "Let the agent do the rest: X and Y" and "do all the remaining parts in one go" handed over only one part, whose agent then said the others "were not implemented in this part". Now every part the request names (or every part still open, for "the rest") is shown back in one line, each as a handoff or a delegation with its reason, one Enter runs them in order, one run per part, and a handoff's review comes before the next part runs. Catching a planted check on one part goes on to the next. A part you exclude ("…, but not the letter", "the rest except the letter") is left out, and a new request for one part drops an older queue, so it never runs on a later OK.
- **"So what have I actually shown here?" shows the whole folder.** It showed only the current part's ideas, so ideas passed earlier vanished ("0 passed a check" after two passes). It now shows the folder's record, as `/why` does outside a task.
- **The step history counts steps you took.** "What has led to a pass so far: asking what it's for, 1 of 1 times" credited a step whenever a pass followed it, though passes come from judging candidates and the step may never have been taken. A step now counts only when you wrote about its concept, or opened or searched a source on it, before the verdict; the angle Pathfinder leans on is counted the same way.
- **A quick check's verdict is shown back too.** A plain "Right. …" in a quick check was judged at once, while the same words on a task's candidate asked "Judge this as your verdict on the candidate? … Enter judges". Both now wait for one Enter (§15); Esc keeps the message as a note and the check waits. An exam answer still goes straight to the Judge.
- **"… check me" after an explanation checks it.** A long explanation ending in "Check me" was answered as a question ("Where to look:"). A message that ends by asking to be checked now logs the explanation and writes a candidate, whatever the decision model read in it.
- **Less noise in the status lines.** A run of the agent's lookups said `2 searches (1 failed)` or `listed 1 folder (2 failed)` with no word on what failed. Failed lookups are the agent's own probes (a wrong path, a bad pattern), so the line now counts only what worked, a failed call's row says why in one line when you expand it, and a run where nothing worked says `2 lookups didn't work (a wrong path or pattern)`. The rule kept `⧗ your OK (Enter runs · Esc cancels)` while the agent was already working on what you'd confirmed; it now goes as soon as you answer, and is never shown while a reply runs.
- **Replies stay in the language you write in.** On a German system an English conversation about an English bill sometimes got its candidate, and some replies, in German. The candidate's model was never told a language and guessed from what it had (the time zone, the workspace path), and the onboarding model could switch the conversation's language on any message, "ok" included. Now the language follows your own messages, read by code (quotes and code left out), and stays until a message clearly in another language changes it; the model's reading only fills it in before your words have. Every model that writes to you (onboarding, the map, the Tutor, candidates and exam questions, the Judge, `/tell`, the agent's report) is told that language, and never to take it from the locale, the time zone, file names or the sources. Files the agent writes follow the task.
- **The agent's commands can't reach Pathfinder's own files.** Sandboxed commands could read the database (the ledger, concept states and planted flaws), its `-wal`, the logs, the model cache and other reviews' staging copies, and write to them when they sat in the workspace. Now one list names everything private to Pathfinder (every folder under every name it can have, `PATHFINDER_HOME`, and the database and what sits beside it wherever `PATHFINDER_DB_PATH` puts it), and both sandboxes hide all of it, for reading and writing, while a staged handoff still sees its own copy. On macOS writes to credential folders inside the workspace are refused too. On Linux commands run in their own process namespace, so they can't read other programs' arguments or stop Pathfinder. The agent's file tools refuse the same files, and `ls`, `glob` and `grep` no longer list or search them when they sit in the workspace (a database set there, its staging copies, a link into a private folder); links that lead out of the workspace are left out too, as `read` already refused them. Both sandboxes go by path, so the folders above a hidden path that a command could rename (in the workspace or a temp folder) are pinned: renaming them no longer carries the database or `auth.json` to a name nothing hides. On Linux Pathfinder's folders and the staging root are made (this user's only) before a command, so one made while it runs stays hidden and a command can't plant a link where one will be; the TUI log and the update stamp aren't written through a link. A linked database's `-wal` and `-shm` beside its target are hidden too, as is the older `tui.log.1`. The startup check runs the whole policy: where it can't be applied there is no sandbox and every command asks. The eval database and long Claude prompts moved from the temp folder into Pathfinder's state folder, and the planted flaw's syntax check into the staging folder. CI now runs the sandbox tests on Linux and macOS instead of skipping them.
- **Commitments in plain words are shown back first.** "I already know this", "just tell me", "let the agent do it" and a verdict in your own words now work like `/known`, `/tell`, `/handoff`/`/delegate` and a typed verdict, with one line saying what will be recorded. A claim is recorded at once and Esc (or `/undo`, or "take that back") withdraws it in the very next message. Asking to be told shows its answer once it is whole, gated like `/tell`; Esc while it's coming records nothing. Handing a part to the agent (handoff for an understood part, delegate otherwise) and a verdict wait for Enter (`/confirm`); Esc (`/cancel`) drops them, and a dropped verdict is kept as a note. When Pathfinder can't tell whether you meant one of these, it asks which in plain words and records nothing (see below). Typed commands run as before.
- **A short claim is asked about, in plain words, for the part it names.** "I understand the part in Folie 1 page 19" or "I get it" was logged with a bare "Logged." when the decision service read it as a log entry, even at 47%. Now a short bare claim, and any message where the decision service is below 0.6 and leans on a commitment (0.4 of its belief in all, or 0.25 on one), gets a question with numbered choices and no commands: record that you already know the part (no check), check you on it, just tell you, let the agent do it, or just a note. Answer with the number or a few words. "I don't know", "don't record anything" or "weiß nicht" never records a claim: the question is dropped and the message goes on as usual. The claim goes on the part your words name, matched by code from its file ("Folie 1" for `FUB_Folien1.pdf`), page, title or concepts, with a question when it could be several; undoing it puts you back where you were.
- **Your explanations get a reply and one next step.** A log entry no longer gets only "Logged.": the reply names what you did in one sentence, in your own words (code checks that every content word comes from your message or the part's title, else it falls back to "Logged."), and ends with one exact next step whose open question the Tutor wrote from the part and its source (one sentence ending in "?", else a fixed one). The Extractor still runs. The `step` event now says whether the question was the Tutor's or a fixed one.
- **One pointer, inside the part.** A part's introduction no longer shows a `Read:` line next to a `Next:` line that points elsewhere. The next step's place stays within the part's own pages (from its page to just before the next part's page in the same deck, at most 3 pages on), else the part's own pointer, so it never sends you to a neighbouring part's slide. Fixed questions read as sentences with any concept name ('What role does “Plane waves, standing waves and EM boundary conditions” play in this part? Put it in your own words.'), and a part with no concepts yet is asked what it is for.
- **No commands taught in replies to plain words.** The part prompt ("Think out loud here. /summary · /known · /delegate") is now a plain invitation, a claim's show-back says "Say undo, or press Esc, to take it back.", and a proposed map change ends with "Say yes to add it, or no."
- **Math on PDF slides reads right.** On slides whose math is in a Symbol font mapped to private-use codes, ρ and ∂ came out as stray symbols (`div D = ✈`, `⊄B`). Pathfinder now asks poppler which font drew each code and maps it back (Symbol: Greek, operators, arrows, bracket pieces; Wingdings: bullets and arrows), and maps Word's Cambria Math dots above, vector arrows and ∑ back from the unrelated scripts they land in. A page whose math is still garbled gets "Math on this page didn't extract cleanly; check it in your PDF viewer: page N" in `/open`, and only its plain prose can be quoted or found. A page where a math font's glyphs came out as plain letters (`25 μm` as `25 mm`) gets the note, and only prose clear of those letters can be quoted (see "Citations hold on math pages"). A lone ∂, −, ∞ or arrow on a line no longer marks a page. A page label set far to the right (`FuB 1.19`) is no longer split when the TUI wraps it.
- **Undo never makes your record look better.** Only a claim can be withdrawn. Undo appends a `retracted` event (the log stays append-only), every state ignores the withdrawn claim, and `/why` lists it as withdrawn. A judged verdict, a review, a handoff, a delegation and an answer you were told can't be undone. Older versions don't know the `retracted` and `step` events and skip them, so they still count a withdrawn claim.
- **An exact next step and a loose horizon.** A part's introduction now ends with `Horizon:` (what it needs, with their status, and what it leads to) and `Next:` (the exact file and lines to read and one open question). Code picks a wide step for an idea you haven't shown and a narrow one for a shaky idea; "give me an example first" or "start from what it's for" changes it, and the message is still logged and answered. Pathfinder leans on the kinds of step that led you to passes, and `/why` shows all of that history ("What has led to a pass so far: …"), along with how many of its ideas passed a check and how many rest on your word alone. No next step is shown while a candidate or review waits. The side panel marks what each part needs (`← 1, 2`).
- **The TUI answers what waits for your OK.** With nothing typed, Enter runs it and Esc cancels it, or takes back a claim you just made; the side panel shows `⧗ Waiting for your OK` with what Enter does, or `↶ You claimed …` while Esc can take it back, and the input's hint says the same. A menu closes and a running reply stops before Esc does anything else, and a draft is never sent or cleared.
- **Tighter around secrets.** `/tell` is held and gated while any secret is active, a waiting review included, not only a candidate. The Judge gets no tools, only your verdict and the files you attached. During a staged handoff the agent's thinking and tool output stay hidden too. An objection that misses a planted flaw now shows you the planted hunk before going back to the agent. A judged verdict is saved before the next candidate is written, so a failure there can't leave it to be judged twice. A verdict waiting for Enter is dropped by a typed command that changes things (`/summary`, `/plan`, …) and is judged only against the candidate it was shown for. A streamed `/tell` answer is recorded as told once its first words arrive, so stopping it after reading leaves the mark. A map change the Tutor proposes while a secret is active passes the gate together with its reply, and is dropped when the reply is held. A waiting candidate is hidden from the Mapper on `/replan` too, as from the Tutor and `/tell`.
- **Plain words reach the part you name, and the review.** "Let the agent write the letter" always proposed delegating the part you were on, even when you had shown every concept the letter rests on, so handoff and review were out of reach in plain words. Now a request for the agent goes to the part it names (by title, a word of it like "the letter", or its number) and proposes a handoff with your review when that review would count (the part is understood, or every concept it rests on is shown or solid); otherwise a delegation, with one line saying which concepts you haven't shown. "Without me checking" still delegates. "I already know the first two parts" or "parts 1 and 2" claims each part, shown back in one line and undone together. "So what do I actually understand now?" or "how am I doing?" shows `/why` instead of sources. Asking Pathfinder to write something ("can you write the letter?") offers it to the agent instead of a told answer saying the workspace is read-only. Right after the agent did a part, the next part no longer proposes a map change for gaps that run didn't open.
- **Approve or object to the agent's result in plain words.** "Looks good, approve it" left the result waiting: only `/approve` and `/object` reached it, and the words were logged on the part instead. Now, while a result waits for your review, an approval or what's wrong with it is shown back and runs on Enter as `/approve` or `/object` would, checked review included. A request for the agent in the message that starts the map is proposed right under the map instead of being dropped.
- **A review stays a review.** `/handoff` on an open part whose concepts you'd all shown left a candidate waiting on it, and a later verdict on that candidate ended the review without recording it; the candidate is now dropped with the handoff, and a verdict never moves a part that has gone on. A review's diffs (one of them planted, if it is checked) are hidden from the Tutor, `/tell` and the Mapper until your verdict. `/why` no longer lists a review that missed its planted check as an approved, reviewable one. The workspace overview given to onboarding and the map follows the same rule as `ls`: no links out of the workspace and nothing private to Pathfinder. On macOS and Windows, Pathfinder's private files are refused in any letter case, including ones that don't exist yet (a database's `-journal`).
- **A wrong belief closes only when you catch it.** Passing an exam question (or any candidate with no planted flaw) on a concept with an open misconception closed the misconception, though nothing checked that belief. Now only passing the flawed candidate built to embody it closes it; the other passes still count for the concept. An exam question no longer carries the belief into its prompt.
- **`/why` says plainly what it counts, and keeps the agent's work.** "Passes: 2 · claims: 4." counted events, so claiming an idea twice, or claiming one you later passed, gave numbers that didn't match the ideas listed; now the line counts the ideas drawn: `Of the ideas above, 1 passed a check and 1 rest on your word alone.` The step history reads in plain words and only shows what a verdict followed (`What has led to a pass so far: starting from an example, 1 of 1 times`), instead of `How you got here: wide·purpose 0 of 0 led to a pass`. A part you handed off or delegated no longer vanishes from `/why` once you move on: it is listed under **Handed to the agent** as `reviewable` or `delegated`.
- **Smaller things from a recorded session.** A quick check's "Quick check:" line comes with its candidate, after the files read for it, instead of standing alone above them. A file the agent writes is named relative to the workspace in its tool line (`letter.txt: 25 lines, was 0`), not by its full path in the staging copy. Enter and Esc on something waiting for your OK show your answer in the thread (`yes, delegate it`, `no`) instead of `/confirm` and `/cancel`. Claiming a part whose concepts you've already shown no longer says "A claim, not evidence": the line names only the concepts resting on your word, or says the part stands on your checks.
- **The model list keeps itself current.** When it is empty or a day old, the TUI fetches it in the background at startup, without waiting, and the pickers show the new models as soon as they arrive. A fresh install no longer starts with no models until `pathfinder models --refresh`.
- **Claude Code and Codex appear by themselves** when their programs are installed, no `models` list needed: `claude-haiku`, `claude-sonnet`, `claude-opus`, and `codex-<model>` (the newest of each kind your ChatGPT plan offers). Threads that saved `codex-luna` or `codex-sol` keep their model.
- **The model pickers are grouped by account and say who pays**: ChatGPT sign-in · ChatGPT plan, Claude Code · Claude subscription, Codex CLI · ChatGPT plan, Anthropic API · pay per use, OpenAI API · pay per use, and your servers. `pathfinder models` prints the same groups, the settings list names each model's account, and the footer marks pay-per-use models with `$`.
- **No silent model switch.** When the model a thread or your defaults ask for isn't available, the status line says which one runs instead and why (not listed yet, list refresh failed, or not offered), a turn keeps the models it started with, and `/status` shows configured and used models for both tiers.
- **Signing in takes two short steps, not a numbered prompt.** `pathfinder auth login` asks how to connect: sign in with your ChatGPT plan (`✓ signed in as …`), use an API key (pay per use), or add your own server. For a key you then pick the provider from a short list with a filter line above it: Anthropic, OpenAI and your servers, each with a word or two of state (`✓ stored`, `✓ from $ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `not configured`), and `(n/N)` under a long list (at most eight rows show, so the conversation stays in view). The filter matches names only, and a key pasted into it is dropped, never shown. An arrow marks the selection; ↑↓ move, Enter picks, Esc goes back a step (also while ChatGPT's browser sign-in waits, where `c` copies the address) and cancels with "Nothing changed." at the first. Keys go into a masked line and are checked by listing that provider's models before they are kept; a key already in its environment variable is offered first. Adding your own server asks its URL, name and key with the same line editing.
- **`/login` and `/logout` inside Pathfinder** show the same panel at the bottom, in place of the input, so the conversation stays in view; `/login` opens by itself when no account works yet. Errors that said to run `pathfinder auth` in a terminal now point at `/login`.
- **Claude Code and Codex aren't login rows**: Pathfinder never signs in there. The model picker's Claude Code and Codex CLI headings say where their own login is.
- **The gate's key moved to Settings** (Ctrl+P → Gate key, next to Decision service): set, replace or remove it there, masked and checked. `pathfinder auth login typesafe`, `auth set typesafe` and `auth remove typesafe` still work for scripts.
- **`pathfinder auth logout [account]`** removes one sign-in or key and nothing else, after asking (a list of what `/login` can create when no account is named; a stored key is listed even while its environment variable is used; a server can lose its key or go altogether). `pathfinder auth status` lists the accounts under headings (plans, API keys · pay per use, your servers, the gate) with their full state and model counts, nothing cut. For scripts: `pathfinder auth login <chatgpt|anthropic|openai|endpoint|typesafe>`, `--with-api-key` reads the key from stdin, and without a terminal Pathfinder says what to name instead of waiting. `pathfinder auth set` still works.
- **What Pathfinder fetched looks different from what the model said.** `/open` shows `▤ path · lines 20–24 of 391` over the numbered source, `/find` shows `⌕ terms · first 12 of 48 matches` with each file once and its lines under a dim gutter, and `/status` is one block with aligned keys. Notes get a dim `·` instead of yellow, which now means only "your turn" or a warning. `/copy` copies only the model's replies; on a selected source `y` copies the code without line numbers and `Y` adds `path:lines` above it. `/map` rows show each part's status mark and number (`← 1, 2`), and a link whose label already names its file drops the repeated url. Resumed threads show their sources and hits the same way.
- **The Zed agent server is gone.** Pathfinder runs in the terminal; `pathfinder acp` now only says so, and the Zed setup is no longer documented. Threads, the record and settings are unchanged.

## 0.4.0

- **Sources, not answers.** A question gets the places in your folder that address it, each with a short line quoted from there and checked against the file by code; the model's own words are never shown. If the folder has nothing, OpenAlex, arXiv and (with a key) Brave Search are searched the same way. Otherwise the reply says **Not found** and suggests terms.
- **`/tell <question>`** gives a model's answer, recorded as *told*.
- **`/find`, `/open` and `/web`** search the folder, show a file or PDF page, and search online. What you read and search is on the record, never as understanding.
- **`/check`** finds the first step of your work that doesn't hold, from typed steps, a file, or a photo or scan of handwritten work, and quotes it without saying why.
- **Spelling and word forms don't matter in search:** "K-means" finds "kmeans", "Schroedinger" finds "Schrödinger", "clustering" finds "clusters".
- In a task, messages that aren't questions get "Logged." and no comment. A reply held back while you judge a candidate is replaced by a fixed one, no longer rewritten.
- **More languages.** Pathfinder's own lines, including the headers of sourced replies and `/check`, now come in Chinese, Hindi, Spanish, Arabic, French, Bengali, Portuguese, Russian, Urdu, Indonesian, Japanese, Turkish, Korean, Italian and Vietnamese, as well as English and German.
- **`pathfinder auth login` asks how to connect models**: ChatGPT sign-in, an Anthropic or OpenAI key, or any OpenAI-compatible service or server (OpenRouter, Ollama, vLLM, ...), which is checked and added to `config.json`.
- New keys: `pathfinder auth set brave` and `pathfinder auth set openalex`. New settings: `quote_cap` and `sources_search`.

## 0.3.4

- **Updates work while Pathfinder's server is down.** `pathfinder update` and the installer fall back to the same signed release on GitHub. The installer itself is on every GitHub release too.

## 0.3.3

- **Updates show up within a minute.** `pathfinder update` and the installer no longer see an old release list that Cloudflare kept too long; while the server is down they still fall back to the cached copy.
- **The program is now `pathfinder`, not `harness`.** Installs that were updated in place under the old name move to `pathfinder` the first time they run, and `harness` stays as a link to it, so a Zed setting that runs `harness` keeps working. Change it to `pathfinder` when you like.

## 0.3.2

- **The commands you'd expect.** `/quit` (or `/exit`, `/q`), `/help`, `/new` (or `/clear`), `/resume`, `/model`, `/status`, `/copy`, `/thinking`, `/join`, `/data` and `/update`. Typing `/` shows the commands that match; Tab completes. An unknown command says so instead of going to the model.
- **The input edits like a shell:** Ctrl+A, Ctrl+U, Ctrl+K, Ctrl+W, Alt+Backspace, and Ctrl+←/→ to move by a word. Ctrl+letters without a meaning no longer type the letter.
- `/help` shows every key and command, and scrolls.

## 0.3.1

- **Updates stay consistent while caches catch up.** Each release now has its own signed manifest that never changes, and `pathfinder update` checks that one, so a cache that hasn't caught up can only offer an older release, not a broken one.
- **Plainer prompts.** The answer and test prompts no longer describe the user as a student; they work the same for any kind of work.
- A new icon.

## 0.3.0

The command is now `pathfinder`, and this release gets Pathfinder ready for a wider beta.

- **New name on the command line.** Run `pathfinder` (no `tui` needed). Installs from before 0.3 keep working: `harness` still runs, and `pathfinder` is added beside it. Environment variables are now `PATHFINDER_*`; the `HARNESS_*` names still work.
- **Bug reports.** `/bug what happened` (or `pathfinder bug`) builds a report with your settings, the last log lines and the last crash, shows you all of it, and sends it only when you confirm. Without joining the beta it is saved to a file instead.
- **Crashes.** A crash puts your terminal back as it was and writes the details to `crash.log` on your computer. A crash inside one reply ends that reply, not the app.
- **The shared gate is used for the gate only**, and only when you have no decision key of your own. Before, other decisions (reading what a message meant, tagging the agent's changes) could go through it too. Your own keys now always come first.
- **When the gate can't check a reply**, the held-back reply now says why: today's shared checks are used up, or the service couldn't be reached.
- **The welcome screen** separates joining the beta from sharing data, and says what declining means.
- **Privacy notice version 3** covers bug reports, crashes, update results and first-time milestones in usage events, and the download counts the server keeps. If you share data, Pathfinder asks again.
